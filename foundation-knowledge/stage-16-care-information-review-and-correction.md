# Review, Feedback, and Correction Safeguards for Active Care Folder Information

## Purpose of Review and Correction Safeguards
Care-related information may change over time, be incomplete, or be misunderstood when presented digitally. This document defines how the system supports review, feedback, and correction in a way that prioritises safety, accuracy, and learning rather than blame.

The aim is to ensure that information remains appropriate and that risks from outdated or incorrect data are identified early.

## Principle of Shared Responsibility
Accuracy of care information is a shared responsibility between the system, the organisation, and professional users. The system does not assume information is always correct, and users are encouraged to remain critical and reflective.

This reinforces professional accountability rather than blind reliance on digital records.

## Feedback and Concern Raising
The system provides clear routes for users to raise concerns about information that appears unclear, inaccurate, or potentially harmful. Concerns are directed to appropriate governance, clinical, or safeguarding processes rather than being resolved informally within the system.

This ensures issues are handled by the right people with the right authority.

## Review and Validation Processes
Care information introduced into the system is subject to periodic review to confirm that it remains relevant and appropriate. Reviews consider whether information is still needed, whether its presentation remains safe, and whether additional safeguards are required.

Information that no longer meets safety or relevance criteria is withdrawn or restricted.

## Safe Handling of Corrections
When corrections are required, changes are made through controlled processes rather than ad-hoc editing. This prevents silent changes that could confuse users or undermine trust.

Corrections are documented so that learning can be captured and shared.

## Learning and Continuous Improvement
Patterns in feedback, corrections, or misunderstandings are analysed to improve system design, guidance, and governance. This supports continuous improvement rather than reactive fixes.

## Alignment with CQC Expectations
These safeguards demonstrate that the organisation actively manages data quality, user understanding, and learning. They align with CQC expectations for safe, responsive, and well-led services using digital systems.

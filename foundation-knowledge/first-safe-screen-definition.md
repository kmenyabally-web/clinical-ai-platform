first-safe-screen-definition.md
1. Purpose of the First Safe Screen
The system begins with a minimal, read-only screen so that the very first thing a user sees is a clear statement of who they are in the system, what role they hold, and what the system is for, without exposing any care or inspection data.
This supports safety and trust by making it impossible for a first-time or returning user to land directly on sensitive records; they must first pass through a neutral, informational space that explains the boundaries of what they can and cannot do.
For a regulated CQC readiness system, this approach demonstrates regulatory care: the provider can show that users are introduced to the system in a controlled way, with no automatic access to clinical or care content on first contact.
2. Information Displayed
On this first safe screen, the user sees only:
Who they are in the system: a simple statement such as their name or chosen display name, and their sign-in status (for example, “You are signed in”).
Their role: a clear label showing whether they are signed in as staff, manager, inspector, or admin, so they understand the capacity in which they are using the system.
Organisation context: the name of the organisation they belong to (for example, the provider’s name) so they know which organisation’s environment they are in.
Purpose of the system: a brief, plain-English explanation that this system is for CQC readiness, care folder oversight, and inspection evidence, not a full clinical record system.
Safety and access statement: a short message explaining that no care or inspection data is shown on this screen and that access to records is controlled and audited.
3. Information Explicitly Not Displayed
This first screen deliberately excludes:
Any person-level information: no names of people using the service, no lists or counts of people, and no identifiers that could indirectly point to an individual.
Any care or health details: no care plans, risk assessments, daily notes, diagnoses, medications, incidents, safeguarding information, or similar content.
Any inspection evidence: no audit history, no reports, no compliance dashboards, and no overdue/in-date status information.
Any service-level operational data: no ward or service lists, no occupancy figures, no staffing information, no performance metrics.
Any uploaded documents or file references: no links or previews of files, no document names, and no indication that particular documents exist.
Any AI-generated content or automated summaries: no suggestions, risk scores, summaries, or classification outputs.
In short, nothing that could reasonably be considered care, health, or inspection data is visible on this screen.
4. User Actions Permitted
On this screen, the user is allowed to:
View their own status: confirm that they are signed in, see their role, and see which organisation they are operating under.
Acknowledge key safety information: read and, if required, confirm understanding of short statements about privacy, appropriate use, and that activity may be monitored and audited.
Access neutral help content: open help or guidance about how the system works in general (for example, how roles are used or how data is protected), provided that help text contains no live care records or identifiable examples.
Sign out: leave the system safely from this screen without having accessed any sensitive data.
Navigate only to other safe, non-data areas: for example, to a page that shows policies, training information, or contact details for support, again with no care or inspection records.
5. User Actions Prohibited
On this first safe screen, the user is not allowed to:
Open any records: no links or routes to view organisations, services, people, care folders, or documents.
Search: no search bar or search function for names, identifiers, or records of any kind.
Create, edit, or delete anything: no buttons or options to add records, change content, close records, or remove data.
Upload or download files: no way to upload documents, export reports, or download information.
Change settings or permissions: no access to role management, configuration, or administration functions.
Trigger any automated processes: no actions that would start bulk updates, reminders, or AI processing of existing data.
In essence, all data-bearing and system-changing actions are intentionally blocked from this first screen.
6. CQC and Safety Alignment
Safety: By showing only identity, role, and high-level context, the first screen ensures that a sign-in error or role misconfiguration cannot immediately lead to unsafe viewing or editing of care records. This is consistent with a “safety first” design, where the first interaction has no clinical impact.
Transparency: The screen makes it clear to the user who they are in the system, how they are being treated (by role), and that their activity may be governed and audited. This matches CQC expectations that staff understand the tools they are using and their responsibilities.
Controlled access: The absence of any direct link from this screen to care or inspection data shows that access is staged and intentional, not automatic. Users must move, in later steps, into clearly signposted areas where access controls and audit are in place.
Least privilege: The screen gives the user only what they need to orient themselves (identity, role, context) and nothing more. That is a practical expression of least privilege at the user interface level.
This definition ensures that the very first user experience in the CQC readiness system is as safe and controlled as possible, while still being understandable, transparent, and supportive of good governance.

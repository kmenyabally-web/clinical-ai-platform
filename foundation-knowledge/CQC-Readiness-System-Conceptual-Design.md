# Digital CQC Readiness System: Conceptual Design

**Shared Understanding for Clinical, Compliance, and Technical Teams**

*This document describes what the system is for, who uses it, how it works from a user’s point of view, and what it must never do. It does not describe how it will be built.*

---

## 1. System Purpose

### What This System Exists To Do

The system exists to help care services **keep every person’s care folder complete, up to date, and easy to show to inspectors**. It does this by:

- Holding the **Active Care Folder** for each person in one place, organised in the same way as the gold-standard blueprint (Identity and Consent; Assessment and Planning; Care and Treatment; Risk and Safety; Health and Wellbeing; Involvement and Communication; Incidents, Safeguarding and Complaints; Governance and Quality).
- Showing **what is in place, what is due for review, and what is overdue**, using the same rules that CQC inspectors use.
- **Reminding the right people** when a document needs to be updated or a review is due.
- Giving staff and managers a **clear view of compliance** for each person and for the service as a whole.
- Making it **quick to find** the documents inspectors usually ask for, and the information needed in an emergency.

The system does **not** deliver care. It helps people **record, organise, and review** the evidence of care so that the folder is always inspection-ready and safe for the person.

---

### The Primary Problem It Solves

Care folders are often:

- **Incomplete** (documents missing or not started).
- **Out of date** (reviews overdue, risk assessments not updated after incidents).
- **Hard to navigate** (inspectors and staff cannot quickly find key evidence).
- **Scattered** (paper in one place, notes elsewhere, no single view of “what’s due”.

As a result, services find it difficult to **prove** that they are safe, effective, caring, responsive, and well-led, even when the care itself is good. The system solves this by making the **state of the folder** visible, by applying the **same compliance rules** the regulator uses, and by **prompting action** so that gaps are closed before an inspection or an emergency.

---

### Who It Is For

The system is for everyone who has a stake in the care folder and in CQC readiness:

- **Frontline staff** (support workers, nurses, key workers) who record care and update documents day to day.
- **Clinicians and therapists** who add treatment records and clinical information.
- **Key workers and care co-ordinators** who own care plans and risk assessments and lead reviews.
- **Managers** who need to see compliance across the service and ensure reviews and audits happen.
- **Compliance and quality leads** who need to prepare for inspection and demonstrate that the service is well-led.
- **Inspectors** (read-only), who need to see the right evidence quickly and in a familiar structure.

It is **not** for the person using the service in place of their own records or communication; the design may later support person-held or person-accessible information, but that is a separate decision.

---

### What Success Looks Like

Success means:

- **Every person has a complete Active Care Folder** that matches the blueprint: all mandatory documents are present, and conditional documents appear when the rules say they are required.
- **Nothing is overdue without a reason**: reviews happen on time, or there is a clear exception (e.g. planned delay agreed by a manager) that can be explained to an inspector.
- **Staff know what to do next**: they see what is due for review and who is responsible, and they receive timely reminders.
- **Inspectors can find evidence quickly**: the folder is organised by section, key documents are easy to locate, and an “inspection view” highlights what inspectors usually check first.
- **Emergency information is one step away**: allergies, medication, key risks, contacts, communication needs, evacuation plan, and resuscitation status are easy to open and read in under a minute.
- **Managers and compliance leads have a true picture**: they can see compliance by person and by document type, spot patterns (e.g. many risk assessments due in the same month), and act before things become overdue.

When this is true, the service can confidently show that it is **governed well** and that **care is documented and reviewed in line with CQC expectations**.

---

## 2. What the System IS (and IS NOT)

### What the System DOES Support

- **Organising** the Active Care Folder so that every document has a place and the structure matches the blueprint and compliance rules.
- **Showing status** of each document: present or missing; in date, due for review, or overdue; and whether it is mandatory or conditional for this person.
- **Reminding** the right role when a review is due or when an event (e.g. incident, change in risk) means a document must be updated.
- **Surfacing** emergency-critical information (allergies, medication, key risks, contacts, communication, evacuation, resuscitation) so it can be found immediately.
- **Giving a compliance view** for one person or for the whole service, so managers can see what is on track and what needs attention.
- **Preparing for inspection** by highlighting documents inspectors usually check first and by offering a simple “inspection view” that follows the same structure inspectors expect.
- **Keeping a clear record** of when documents were last updated and when they are next due, so that “know on the day” is easier for staff and managers.

The system **supports** safe, lawful, and well-documented care by making the folder complete, current, and findable.

---

### What the System Does NOT Replace

The system does **not** replace:

- **Clinical or professional judgement.** Decisions about what care to give, what risks to take, and what to record remain with the responsible professional. The system may suggest that a risk assessment is due; it does not decide what the risks are or how to manage them.
- **Electronic Patient Records (EPRs) or clinical systems.** Where a service uses an EPR for clinical notes, prescriptions, or care plans, the system does not duplicate or replace that. It may point to where information lives or show what must be in place for CQC; the decision about how to link or integrate with the EPR is separate.
- **Professional accountability.** The responsible role (nurse, key worker, manager, etc.) remains accountable for the quality and accuracy of the content. The system helps them stay on top of deadlines and structure; it does not take over their duty to assess, plan, and review properly.
- **Human oversight.** Managers and compliance leads must still interpret the picture, prioritise, and decide what to do. The system informs; it does not decide.
- **The need for training and culture.** Good documentation and CQC readiness depend on staff knowing why it matters and how to do it. The system supports that; it does not substitute for training or a learning culture.
- **Legal or regulatory advice.** The system applies the compliance rules as set out in the ruleset; it does not give legal or regulatory advice. Complex cases (e.g. capacity, deprivation of liberty) still need qualified professional input.

In short: the system **supports** people to do the right thing at the right time; it does **not** do the job for them.

## 3. User Roles

Each role is defined in terms of **who they are** and **what they use the system for**. The same person may have more than one role (e.g. a nurse who is also a key worker).

---

### Frontline Staff (Support Workers, Care Workers)

**Who they are:** Staff who deliver day-to-day care and support: personal care, activities, meals, and general support. They may work in a person’s home, a care home, or a ward.

**What they use the system for:**

- Recording **daily care notes** and **activity and engagement** so that what was done is documented.
- Checking **emergency-critical information** (e.g. allergies, communication needs, evacuation plan) before or during a shift.
- Seeing **what is in the person’s folder** so they know what the plan is and what to record.
- Being reminded when **their** recordings are due (e.g. daily notes, food and fluid record if in use).

They do **not** usually own care plans or risk assessments; they feed into the folder and need a simple, clear way to record and to see what matters for safety.

---

### Key Workers

**Who they are:** The staff member (or small team) who has the main relationship with the person and who co-ordinates their care plan, reviews, and involvement. They may be a support worker, nurse, or care co-ordinator depending on the service.

**What they use the system for:**

- Keeping the **care and support plan** and **personalised care plan** up to date and ensuring **reviews** happen on time.
- Owning and updating **risk assessments** (and, where applicable, self-harm, violence, or missing person risk) and seeing **reminders** when a review is due or when an event (e.g. incident) means an update is required.
- Recording **involvement and participation** (reviews, meetings, feedback) and **relatives and carer contact**.
- Keeping **who to contact** and **communication passport** (or equivalent) current.
- Seeing the **compliance status** for their people so they can prioritise what to do next.

They are the main “owner” of the folder for each person and use the system to stay on top of structure, deadlines, and evidence.

---

### Nurses and Clinicians

**Who they are:** Registered nurses, therapists, consultants, or other clinicians who deliver or oversee treatment, medication, and clinical decisions.

**What they use the system for:**

- Recording **treatment** (e.g. therapy sessions, clinical interventions) and **medication** administration and review.
- Updating **allergies and adverse reactions**, **physical health** checks, and (where applicable) **health passport**, **diet and nutrition plan**, and **DNAR or ReSPECT**.
- Ensuring **consent and capacity** (and best interests where needed) are documented and reviewed when care or circumstances change.
- Seeing **risk** and **restraint** information so clinical decisions are informed by the full picture.
- Being reminded when **medication reviews**, **physical health checks**, or **capacity** reviews are due.

They use the system to keep clinical evidence in the right place and on time, without the system making clinical decisions for them.

---

### Managers

**Who they are:** Team leaders, service managers, or locality managers who are responsible for the quality and safety of the service and for CQC readiness.

**What they use the system for:**

- Seeing **compliance across the service**: which people have complete folders, what is due or overdue, and where the main gaps are.
- Ensuring **reviews and audits** happen: care plan reviews, risk assessment reviews, care record audits, and (where applicable) DoLS/LPS, safeguarding, and incident follow-up.
- Allocating work: seeing **who is responsible** for each document and **what is due** so they can support key workers and clinicians to prioritise.
- Checking **incidents, safeguarding, and complaints** are recorded and that **lessons learned** and actions are tracked.
- Preparing for **inspection**: using the inspection view and compliance summary to brief staff and to know where to focus last-minute checks.

They use the system to **oversee** compliance and to **lead** improvement, not to replace their judgement.

---

### Compliance and Quality Leads

**Who they are:** Staff with a dedicated role in quality, compliance, or CQC preparation. They may sit in a single service or across several.

**What they use the system for:**

- Monitoring **service-wide compliance** and trends (e.g. many risk assessments due in one month, or repeated overdue care plan reviews).
- Running or tracking **care plan and care record audits** and ensuring **audit logs** and actions are visible.
- Preparing **inspection packs** or **inspection view**: making sure the right evidence is easy to find and that staff know what inspectors usually ask for.
- Supporting **managers and key workers** with guidance on the compliance rules and on what “strong” evidence looks like.

They use the system as the **single source of truth** for “are we ready?” and “where are we weak?”.

---

### Inspectors (Read-Only)

**Who they are:** CQC inspectors (or others acting in an inspection role) who need to look at care folders to assess whether the service is safe, effective, caring, responsive, and well-led.

**What they use the system for:**

- Viewing the **Active Care Folder** in the same structure they expect (sections A–H).
- Finding **key documents quickly**: care plan, risk assessment, daily notes, medication, consent, incidents and safeguarding, review dates.
- Seeing **emergency-critical** information if they need to understand how the service supports safety in a crisis.
- Checking **dates and completeness** (e.g. when the care plan was last reviewed, whether risk assessment is current).

They have **read-only** access. They do not create or edit content; the system is there to make evidence **easy to find and understand** so that inspection is fair and efficient.

---

## 4. Core System Components

The system is described here as a set of **components**: major parts that users would recognise and that each have a clear purpose. This is conceptual only; how they are built is not specified.

---

### Active Care Folder (Per Person)

**What it is:** The digital equivalent of the person’s care folder, organised into the eight sections from the blueprint (Identity and Consent; Assessment and Planning; Care and Treatment; Risk and Safety; Health and Wellbeing; Involvement and Communication; Incidents, Safeguarding and Complaints; Governance and Quality).

**Purpose:** To hold all the documents and information that together show who the person is, what they need, what care they receive, how risks are managed, and how the service involves them and learns from incidents. Everything has a place; nothing is “floating”. Staff and inspectors can find evidence by section and by document type.

**What users see:** A clear list of sections; under each section, the expected document types; for each document, whether it exists, is in date, due for review, or overdue; and who is responsible. Optional: a short “emergency summary” that brings together the seven emergency-critical items in one view.

---

### Compliance Status Indicators

**What it is:** A simple way to show, for each document and for the folder as a whole, whether the service is **on track** or **needs action**.

**Purpose:** So that staff and managers can see at a glance what is complete and current and what is missing or overdue. Status is based on the compliance rules (in date, due, overdue; mandatory vs conditional; event-triggered when applicable).

**What users see:** For each document: a status (e.g. in date / due / overdue / not started) and, where relevant, the next review date and the responsible role. For the person: an overall picture (e.g. “complete”, “some items due”, “items overdue”). For the service: a summary of how many people have complete folders, how many have overdue items, and which document types are most often overdue.

---

### Review and Alert Mechanisms

**What it is:** The way the system **reminds** the right person that something is due or that an event has happened that should trigger an update.

**Purpose:** To close the gap between “we know we should review it” and “we actually do it”. The system applies the review cycle rules (e.g. care plan every 12 months, risk assessment after an incident) and tells the responsible role when action is needed.

**What users see:** Reminders or alerts that say what is due (e.g. “Risk assessment for [person] due for review” or “Care plan review overdue”). The alert goes to the role that is accountable (e.g. key worker, manager). Optionally, “due soon” as well as “overdue” so people can plan. Event-triggered reminders (e.g. “An incident was recorded for [person]; risk assessment and incident review must be updated”) so that the rules are applied consistently.

---

### Emergency Summary (or Emergency View)

**What it is:** One place where the seven emergency-critical items are shown together: allergies and adverse reactions; current medication list; key risks and safety plan summary; who to contact; communication passport or key communication needs; personal emergency evacuation plan; resuscitation/DNAR status.

**Purpose:** So that in an emergency (collapse, missing person, fire, crisis), any staff member—including someone who does not know the person—can find and read this information in under a minute. The system does not create the content; it **surfaces** it in one view that is easy to open and read.

**What users see:** A single screen or page with these seven areas, in plain language, with the most up-to-date information the service holds. No need to open multiple documents or search.

---

### Inspection View

**What it is:** A way of presenting the Active Care Folder (and optionally the service-level compliance summary) that is **optimised for inspection**: same structure as the blueprint, key documents easy to find, and (if desired) a short list of “documents inspectors usually check first”.

**Purpose:** To help the service show evidence quickly and in the order and structure inspectors expect. This reduces stress on staff and speeds up inspection. It does not hide or alter evidence; it **organises** it for the inspection context.

**What users see:** The same folder content, but arranged or highlighted so that care plan, risk assessment, daily notes, medication, consent, incidents and safeguarding, and review dates are easy to open. Optionally, a one-page “compliance summary” for the service (e.g. how many people, how many folders complete, last audit date) that managers can use to brief inspectors.

---

### Service-Wide Compliance View

**What it is:** A view for managers and compliance leads that shows **all people** in the service (or a chosen part of it) and the **overall state** of compliance: how many folders are complete, how many have overdue items, which document types are most often missing or overdue, and when the next “wave” of reviews is due.

**Purpose:** So that leadership can see the true picture: not just one person’s folder but the whole service. This supports prioritisation (e.g. “we have 20 risk assessments due this month”), resource allocation, and honest preparation for inspection.

**What users see:** Summaries and simple lists: e.g. “12 people with overdue care plan review”, “5 people with risk assessment due this week”, “last care record audit: 3 months ago”. No need to open each folder to see the pattern.

---

### Audit and Review Trail

**What it is:** A way to record and see **when** documents were last updated, **when** reviews were done, and **when** audits (e.g. care plan audit, care record audit) were carried out.

**Purpose:** So that the service can prove that reviews and audits happen on time and that the folder is not only complete but **current**. This supports “know on the day” for staff and satisfies inspectors that governance is in place.

**What users see:** For each document (or document type): last updated date, last review date, next review date, and (where applicable) who did it. For the service: audit log with date, scope (e.g. “care plans for Ward A”), and main findings or actions.

---

## 5. End-to-End Usage Flow

These flows describe **what happens** from the user’s point of view. They are not technical; they are the steps people take and what the system does to support them.

---

### When Care Is Delivered Day to Day

1. Staff start their shift and may open the **Active Care Folder** (or the **emergency summary**) for each person they are supporting, to check allergies, communication needs, and key risks.
2. During the shift they deliver care and activities as planned. They use the system to **record** what was done: daily care notes, activity and engagement, and (if applicable) food and fluid, sleep or behaviour chart, or medication administration.
3. The system **stores** these in the right section (Care and Treatment; Health and Wellbeing as appropriate) so that the folder stays up to date.
4. The **compliance status** for that person may update (e.g. “daily note present for today”). If something is still due (e.g. a review), the status continues to show it so the key worker or manager can see it later.
5. At the end of the shift, staff know they have recorded what was required; the next day, the same cycle repeats.

**Outcome:** The folder reflects what actually happened. Inspectors and managers can see that care is documented and that emergency information is available.

---

### When Documents Are Updated or Reviewed

1. The system **reminds** the responsible role (e.g. key worker) that a document is **due for review** (e.g. care plan in 3 months, or risk assessment due now).
2. The key worker (or nurse, or manager) **opens** the relevant document in the folder, reviews the content, updates it with the person (and family where appropriate), and **saves** or **signs off** the review.
3. The system **records** the date of review and, where applicable, sets the **next review date** based on the compliance rules (e.g. care plan: next review in 12 months).
4. The **compliance status** for that document and for the person updates to “in date”. Any **alert** for that item is cleared or marked done.
5. If the person’s **needs or risks change**, the responsible role updates the care plan or risk assessment as appropriate; the system may **prompt** an event-triggered review (e.g. “Risk assessment should be updated after this incident”) so the rule is not forgotten.

**Outcome:** Reviews happen on time, evidence is current, and the service can show that plans and risks are regularly reviewed and owned.

---

### When Risks or Incidents Occur

1. Something happens: an **incident** (e.g. fall, behaviour, concern) or a **safeguarding** concern, or **restraint** is used.
2. The staff member (or manager) **records** the incident or concern in the system in the right section (Incidents, Safeguarding and Complaints; or Care and Treatment for restraint).
3. The system **applies the event-triggered rules**: it reminds the responsible role that the **risk assessment** (and, if applicable, self-harm, violence, or safeguarding risk) **must be reviewed** and that an **incident investigation** and **lessons learned** may be required.
4. The manager or key worker **updates** the risk assessment and completes the investigation and any action plan. The system **records** that these are done and when.
5. If **restraint** was used, the system ensures the **restraint record** is completed and that a **restraint incident review** is due; the responsible role completes it so that reduction can be planned.
6. **Compliance status** may show “action required” until the risk assessment and incident follow-up are complete; once they are, status returns to on track.

**Outcome:** Incidents and risks lead to the right updates and reviews, so the folder stays accurate and the service can show that it learns and acts.

---

### When an Inspection Is Announced

1. The service is **notified** that CQC will inspect (or that a visit is planned).
2. Managers and compliance leads open the **service-wide compliance view** and the **inspection view** to see:
   - How many people have complete folders and how many have overdue items.
   - Which document types are most often missing or overdue.
   - When the last care record audit was and what it found.
3. They **prioritise**: they may focus on closing the most critical gaps (e.g. overdue risk assessments, overdue care plan reviews, missing consent or DoLS) and on briefing staff on “know on the day” (when was the care plan reviewed, who is responsible, where is the emergency summary).
4. Key workers and nurses use **reminders and compliance status** to complete any outstanding reviews or recordings before the inspection.
5. The **inspection view** is prepared (or confirmed): key documents are easy to find, and staff know where to show inspectors the care plan, risk assessment, daily notes, medication, consent, incidents and safeguarding, and review dates.
6. **Emergency summary** is checked so that allergies, medication, contacts, communication, PEEP, and DNAR are correct and easy to show if asked.

**Outcome:** The service enters the inspection with a true picture of compliance, with gaps closed where possible and with staff confident about where evidence lives and what they can say.

---

### During an Inspection

1. Inspectors (with **read-only** access) open the **inspection view** or the **Active Care Folder** for the people they have chosen to look at.
2. They **navigate** by section (A–H) and by document type, in the same structure as the blueprint. They can open **care plan**, **risk assessment**, **daily notes**, **medication**, **consent**, **incidents and safeguarding**, and **review dates** without searching.
3. Staff may **guide** them (“the care plan is here; it was last reviewed on [date]”) and use the same view so that what the inspector sees is what the service uses every day.
4. If the inspector asks about **emergency** or **safety** (e.g. “How do you know about this person’s allergies?”), staff open the **emergency summary** and show the seven areas in one place.
5. If the inspector asks about **service-wide** governance (e.g. “When did you last audit care plans?”), the manager or compliance lead uses the **service-wide compliance view** and **audit trail** to show when audits were done and what was found.
6. The system **does not change** what is in the folder; it only **presents** it. If something is missing or overdue, that is visible. The purpose is to make evidence easy to find and to show the service in an honest light.

**Outcome:** Inspection is efficient, evidence is found quickly, and the service can demonstrate how it organises and reviews care—whether the result is strong or highlights areas to improve.

## 6. Boundaries and Guardrails

These statements define what the system **must never do**, what **always** stays with people, and how the system **supports** without **controlling** care. They protect both the person using the service and the staff using the system.

---

### What the System Must Never Do

- **Never decide** what care to give, what risks a person has, or what should be in a care plan or risk assessment. It may remind that a review is due; it does not write the content or make the judgement.
- **Never override** professional or clinical judgement. If a professional decides that a review can wait (e.g. person in crisis, best interest to delay), the system may still show “overdue” but it does not force an action; the professional remains accountable for the decision.
- **Never hide or alter** evidence to make the service look better. The inspection view organises and highlights; it does not remove or change documents. Inspectors see the same status (in date, due, overdue) that the service sees.
- **Never replace** the need for qualified advice on capacity, best interests, deprivation of liberty, or safeguarding. The system can show that a DoLS authorisation is in place or that a safeguarding concern was recorded; it does not give legal or regulatory advice.
- **Never record or share** information about the person in a way that is not allowed by law or by the service’s policies (e.g. confidentiality, data protection). How the system is used must follow the same rules as paper records.
- **Never force** a single way of working. Services may use different roles, different review frequencies in line with local policy, or different document names. The system should support the compliance rules while allowing local variation where the rules permit.

---

### What Decisions Always Remain Human-Led

- **What to write** in each document (care plan, risk assessment, daily note, etc.). The system may provide structure and reminders; the content is always written by a person.
- **Whether to delay a review** when there is a good reason (e.g. person unavailable, crisis). The system may flag “overdue”; the manager or key worker decides whether to delay and how to record the reason.
- **How to respond** to an incident or safeguarding concern. The system may remind that risk assessment and investigation are required; the manager and team decide what happened and what to do.
- **Who is responsible** for each person or each document type. The system may suggest a role (e.g. key worker); the service assigns the actual person and can change it.
- **What “good” looks like** in each service. The system applies the compliance rules (in date, due, overdue; mandatory vs conditional); the service decides how to achieve that and how to prioritise when resources are limited.
- **When to involve** the person, family, or advocate. The system may remind that involvement should be recorded at review; the key worker or care co-ordinator decides how to do it and with whom.

---

### How the System Supports, But Does Not Control, Care

- **Supports** by giving a **clear place** for every type of evidence, so that nothing is forgotten and the folder matches what inspectors expect.
- **Supports** by **showing status** (in date, due, overdue) so that staff and managers see the truth and can prioritise.
- **Supports** by **reminding** the right role when something is due or when an event (incident, risk change) should trigger an update, so that the rules are applied consistently.
- **Supports** by **surfacing** emergency-critical information in one view, so that staff can act quickly and safely without searching.
- **Supports** by **giving managers and compliance leads** a service-wide view, so that they can lead improvement and prepare for inspection with facts.
- **Does not control** because it does not write care plans, assess risk, or decide what care to give. It **organises, reminds, and displays**; people **assess, plan, deliver, and review**.

The system is a **governance and readiness tool**. It helps the service stay complete, current, and transparent. It does not run the care; the people who use it do.

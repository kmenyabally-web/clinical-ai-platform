# Decision and Approval Governance for Expanded Data Reads

## Purpose of Decision Governance
In regulated health and social care systems, expanding access to data is a governance decision, not a technical convenience. Clear decision and approval controls ensure that expanded reads occur only through deliberate, accountable action, and not through assumption, drift, or informal agreement.

This document defines how decisions to expand data access are governed, approved, and recorded.

## Separation Between System Capability and Permission
The system may be technically capable of reading more data, but capability alone does not grant permission. Permission to expand access is granted only through formal decision-making, independent of those who built or configured the system.

This separation prevents accidental or unauthorised access driven by technical possibility rather than governance intent.

## Roles Responsible for Approval
Responsibility for approving expanded data reads sits with senior governance, assurance, or information oversight roles. These roles are accountable for data protection, patient safety, and regulatory compliance.

System builders, developers, or operational users cannot self-approve expanded access.

## Decision Inputs
Approval decisions are informed by:
- Evidence that existing controls continue to operate reliably
- Verification and assurance records from earlier stages
- Risk assessments covering safety, confidentiality, and regulatory impact
- Confirmation that expansion aligns with the system’s approved purpose

Decisions are paused if evidence is incomplete or unclear.

## Formal Approval and Recording
All decisions to expand data access are formally recorded. Records include:
- What expansion is approved
- Who approved it and in what role
- The evidence reviewed
- The date and any conditions attached to the approval

No expanded access is permitted without this record.

## Prevention of Informal Expansion
Informal, temporary, or undocumented expansion of access is explicitly prohibited. Access cannot be widened for convenience, testing, troubleshooting, or urgency without following the formal approval process.

This ensures that all access expansion remains visible, reviewable, and accountable.

## Alignment with CQC Expectations
This decision governance model demonstrates that data access is controlled by leadership, evidence, and accountability. It aligns with CQC expectations for well-led services, proactive risk management, and safe digital governance.

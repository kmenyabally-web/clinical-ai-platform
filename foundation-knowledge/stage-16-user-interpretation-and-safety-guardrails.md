# User Interpretation and Safety Guardrails for Active Care Folder Information

## Purpose of Interpretation Guardrails
When care-related information is presented digitally, there is a risk that users may over-interpret, misinterpret, or rely on information in ways that were not intended. This document defines how the system supports safe understanding and appropriate use of Active Care Folder information.

The aim is to ensure that information informs awareness without replacing professional judgement or established care processes.

## Principle of Information, Not Instruction
The system presents care information as descriptive context only. It does not provide instructions, recommendations, or prompts for action. This prevents the system from being perceived as a decision-making authority.

Users are reminded that responsibility for care decisions remains with trained professionals and established organisational processes.

## Clarity of Purpose and Limitations
Information displayed within the system is accompanied by clear statements explaining:
- what the information represents
- what it does not represent
- how it should and should not be used

This clarity reduces the risk of assumption or misuse.

## Avoidance of Implied Clinical Authority
The system avoids language, layout, or visual cues that could imply clinical authority or prioritisation. No risk scores, alerts, or recommendations are presented at this stage.

This prevents users from assigning undue weight to limited information.

## Support for Escalation Through Proper Channels
Where users require clarification or action, the system directs them back to established clinical, safeguarding, or organisational processes rather than attempting to resolve issues within the system itself.

This ensures appropriate escalation and accountability.

## Monitoring for Misuse or Misinterpretation
Use of the system is reviewed to identify patterns that suggest misunderstanding or inappropriate reliance. Where issues are identified, additional guidance or restrictions are applied.

## Alignment with CQC Expectations
These guardrails demonstrate that the organisation recognises human-factor risks in digital systems. They align with CQC expectations for safe practice, professional accountability, and systems that support — rather than replace — human judgement.

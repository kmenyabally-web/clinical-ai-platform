# CQC Readiness: Gold-Standard Active Care Folder Blueprint

**Foundation Knowledge for Digital CQC Readiness**  
*For Mental Health, Learning Disability, and Other Complex Care Services*

---

## Purpose of This Document

This document defines the **authoritative structure and content** of an Active Care Folder—the live record of care for each person using a service. It is written for:

- Service managers and compliance leads
- Staff who maintain and update care records
- Anyone designing or implementing a digital CQC readiness system

It is **not** technical. It describes *what* must be in place and *why*, in terms that regulators and inspectors recognise.

---

## 1. What Is an Active Care Folder?

An **Active Care Folder** (sometimes called the care record, care file, or person-held record) is the **single, up-to-date set of documents** that together show:

- Who the person is and what they need
- What care and treatment they receive
- How risks are managed
- How the service involves the person and those who matter to them
- That the service is safe, effective, caring, responsive, and well-led

For **Mental Health (MH)**, **Learning Disability (LD)**, and **Other Complex Care** services, the folder must support CQC inspection and day-to-day safe care. The same structure can be used across service types, with some documents **conditional** on the type of care (MH, LD, or All).

---

## 2. Gold-Standard Folder Structure: Sections Overview

The folder is organised into **sections**. Each section has a clear purpose. Documents sit under one section only to avoid duplication and to make it easy for staff and inspectors to find evidence.

| Section | Purpose in Simple Terms |
|--------|--------------------------|
| **Section A: Identity and Consent** | Who the person is, who can speak for them, and what consent and capacity decisions have been made. |
| **Section B: Assessment and Planning** | What the service knows about the person’s needs, strengths, and goals, and how care and treatment are planned. |
| **Section C: Care and Treatment** | What is done day to day: care plans, treatment, activities, and how they are recorded and reviewed. |
| **Section D: Risk and Safety** | All risk assessments and safety plans so that risks are understood and managed. |
| **Section E: Health and Wellbeing** | Physical health, medication, and other clinical information that keeps the person well. |
| **Section F: Involvement and Communication** | How the person and people important to them are involved, and how communication needs are met. |
| **Section G: Incidents, Safeguarding and Complaints** | What has gone wrong, how it was handled, and that the service learns and improves. |
| **Section H: Governance and Quality** | Evidence that the service monitors quality, audits records, and acts on findings. |

---

## 3. Document-by-Document Specification

For each document the following is defined:

- **Document name**
- **Mandatory / Conditional / Optional**
- **Applicable service types**: MH (Mental Health), LD (Learning Disability), All
- **Review frequency**: e.g. daily, weekly, monthly, annually, or event-triggered
- **Responsible role**: who is accountable for ensuring the document exists and is up to date

Then each document is mapped to **CQC domain(s)** and **why inspectors expect this evidence**.

---

### Section A: Identity and Consent

| Document Name | Mandatory / Conditional / Optional | Service Types | Review Frequency | Responsible Role |
|---------------|------------------------------------|---------------|------------------|------------------|
| Referral and acceptance record | Mandatory | All | Event-triggered (on admission/referral) | Manager / Admin |
| Consent to care and treatment | Mandatory | All | Event-triggered; re-check when care changes | Nurse / Key Worker / Consultant |
| Capacity assessment and best interests (where applicable) | Conditional (when capacity in doubt) | All | Event-triggered; review when circumstances change | Consultant / Best Interests Assessor |
| Advance decisions / Advance statements (if any) | Optional (if held by person) | All | Event-triggered; review when person requests | Key Worker / Nurse |
| Who to contact (next of kin, advocate, GP) | Mandatory | All | At least annually; update when person says | Key Worker / Nurse |

**CQC mapping – Section A**

- **Safe**: Consent and capacity evidence shows care is lawful and person-centred.
- **Effective**: Care is based on informed consent and, where needed, best interests.
- **Caring**: Respect for identity and choice is visible.
- **Well-Led**: Clear accountability for consent and capacity.

**Why inspectors expect this**: They need to see that the service knows who the person is, who speaks for them, and that care is consented to or properly decided in best interests. Missing or outdated consent is a common finding.

---

### Section B: Assessment and Planning

| Document Name | Mandatory / Conditional / Optional | Service Types | Review Frequency | Responsible Role |
|---------------|------------------------------------|---------------|------------------|------------------|
| Initial assessment (needs, strengths, preferences) | Mandatory | All | On admission; full re-assessment annually | Nurse / Key Worker / Care Co-ordinator |
| Care and support plan (or equivalent) | Mandatory | All | Review at least every 12 months; sooner if needs change | Key Worker / Care Co-ordinator / Nurse |
| Personalised care plan (daily/weekly goals and activities) | Mandatory | All | Weekly or as per plan | Key Worker / Nurse |
| Mental Health Care Plan / Care Programme Approach (CPA) documentation | Conditional (MH) | MH | As per CPA policy (e.g. 6-monthly review minimum) | Care Co-ordinator / Consultant |
| Health Action Plan (or equivalent) | Conditional (LD) | LD | Annual review; update when health changes | Nurse / Key Worker / GP liaison |
| Positive Behaviour Support (PBS) plan (where applicable) | Conditional (LD / complex behaviour) | LD, All | As per plan; formal review at least annually | PBS lead / Behaviour specialist / Key Worker |
| Transition plan (admission, transfer, discharge) | Mandatory | All | Event-triggered | Manager / Key Worker / Care Co-ordinator |

**CQC mapping – Section B**

- **Effective**: Assessments and plans show that needs are identified and care is planned.
- **Caring**: Plans show person-centred goals and preferences.
- **Responsive**: Plans respond to changing needs and life events.
- **Well-Led**: Planning processes are consistent and reviewed.

**Why inspectors expect this**: Inspectors look at whether assessments are current, plans are personalised, and reviews happen. Out-of-date or generic plans are frequently cited.

---

### Section C: Care and Treatment

| Document Name | Mandatory / Conditional / Optional | Service Types | Review Frequency | Responsible Role |
|---------------|------------------------------------|---------------|------------------|------------------|
| Daily care notes / progress notes | Mandatory | All | Daily (or per shift where 24-hour care) | Nurse / Support Worker / Key Worker |
| Activity and engagement record | Mandatory | All | As per plan (e.g. daily or weekly) | Key Worker / Support Worker |
| Treatment record (psychological, therapy, clinical interventions) | Mandatory where treatment given | MH, All | Per session / per care plan | Therapist / Nurse / Consultant |
| Seclusion / long-term segregation record (if applicable) | Conditional (MH/secure) | MH | Per episode; review as per policy | Ward Manager / Responsible Clinician |
| Restraint and restrictive practice record | Mandatory where used | All | Per episode; trend review monthly | Nurse / Manager |
| Food and fluid record (where risk) | Conditional (e.g. eating disorder, underweight) | All | Daily when in use | Nurse / Support Worker |
| Sleep and behaviour chart (where used) | Conditional | MH, LD | As per plan | Nurse / Key Worker |

**CQC mapping – Section C**

- **Safe**: Daily notes and treatment records show what was done and when; restraint/seclusion must be justified and reviewed.
- **Effective**: Treatment and activities are recorded and linked to the care plan.
- **Caring**: Notes should reflect the person’s experience and dignity.
- **Responsive**: Care adapts when needs or risks change.

**Why inspectors expect this**: Inspectors sample daily notes and treatment records to see if care is delivered as planned and documented. Gaps in recording or missing restraint documentation are high-risk findings.

---

### Section D: Risk and Safety

| Document Name | Mandatory / Conditional / Optional | Service Types | Review Frequency | Responsible Role |
|---------------|------------------------------------|---------------|------------------|------------------|
| Risk assessment (combined or multi-domain) | Mandatory | All | At least every 12 months; after incident or change | Key Worker / Nurse / Care Co-ordinator |
| Self-harm and suicide risk assessment (where relevant) | Conditional (MH and where indicated) | MH, All | As per policy (e.g. after incident, on leave, weekly in crisis) | Nurse / Consultant / Care Co-ordinator |
| Violence and aggression risk assessment | Conditional (where risk identified) | MH, LD, All | At least annually; after incident | Nurse / Key Worker / Manager |
| Safeguarding risk assessment | Mandatory where safeguarding concerns | All | When concern raised; review as per plan | Manager / Safeguarding Lead |
| Fire safety and evacuation (personal emergency evacuation plan – PEEP) | Mandatory | All | On admission; review when mobility/premises change | Manager / Fire lead |
| Missing person risk and protocol | Conditional (community/leave) | MH, LD | When risk identified; review with care plan | Key Worker / Nurse / Manager |
| Environmental risk assessment (for the person’s environment) | Mandatory in residential/ward settings | All | On admission; review when environment changes | Manager / Nurse |

**CQC mapping – Section D**

- **Safe**: Core domain. Inspectors expect clear, current risk assessments and plans that are followed.
- **Effective**: Risks are identified and mitigated in line with good practice.
- **Well-Led**: Risk is overseen and reviewed at service level.

**Why inspectors expect this**: Risk is at the heart of “Safe”. Incomplete, outdated or generic risk assessments are among the most common reasons for ratings being limited or inadequate. Inspectors often look at this section early.

---

### Section E: Health and Wellbeing

| Document Name | Mandatory / Conditional / Optional | Service Types | Review Frequency | Responsible Role |
|---------------|------------------------------------|---------------|------------------|------------------|
| Medication record (MAR / prescription chart) | Mandatory where medication given | All | Daily (per administration) | Nurse / Responsible clinician |
| Medication review record | Mandatory | All | As per policy (e.g. 12-monthly minimum; 6-monthly for psychotropics) | GP / Consultant / Pharmacist |
| Physical health check and screening record | Mandatory | All | At least annually; as per LD/MH guidelines | Nurse / GP / Practice nurse |
| Health passport (or equivalent for LD) | Conditional (strongly recommended LD) | LD, All | Update when health or medication changes | Key Worker / Nurse |
| Allergies and adverse reactions | Mandatory | All | Update when identified; checked at each review | Nurse / Key Worker |
| DNAR / ReSPECT form (if applicable) | Optional (when in place) | All | Review as per policy | Consultant / GP / Nurse |
| Diet and nutrition plan (where needed) | Conditional | All | When need identified; review with care plan | Nurse / Dietitian / Key Worker |

**CQC mapping – Section E**

- **Safe**: Medication and allergy documentation prevent harm; physical health reduces premature mortality (especially in MH/LD).
- **Effective**: Regular reviews and screening show proactive health management.
- **Caring**: Health and comfort are prioritised.

**Why inspectors expect this**: Medication errors and poor physical health monitoring are key CQC themes. MAR charts and review dates are routinely checked. In LD services, health passports and annual health checks are expected.

---

### Section F: Involvement and Communication

| Document Name | Mandatory / Conditional / Optional | Service Types | Review Frequency | Responsible Role |
|---------------|------------------------------------|---------------|------------------|------------------|
| Communication passport / profile | Mandatory (LD); recommended others | LD, All | Update when communication needs change | Key Worker / Speech and Language / Nurse |
| Involvement and participation record (reviews, meetings, feedback) | Mandatory | All | Per review; at least evidence of annual involvement | Key Worker / Care Co-ordinator |
| Relatives and carer contact and involvement log | Mandatory | All | Ongoing; summarised at review | Key Worker / Nurse |
| Advocacy referral and contact record | Conditional (when advocate involved) | All | Per contact; review at care plan review | Key Worker / Manager |
| Accessible information record (format, easy read, interpreter) | Mandatory | All | When need identified; review at plan review | Key Worker / Nurse |

**CQC mapping – Section F**

- **Caring**: Involvement and communication show respect and dignity.
- **Responsive**: Services respond to how people communicate and who they want involved.
- **Effective**: Care is improved when the person and family are engaged.

**Why inspectors expect this**: CQC looks for evidence that the person and their network are involved in planning and review. In LD, communication passports are standard. Inspectors may ask to see these early in an inspection.

---

### Section G: Incidents, Safeguarding and Complaints

| Document Name | Mandatory / Conditional / Optional | Service Types | Review Frequency | Responsible Role |
|---------------|------------------------------------|---------------|------------------|------------------|
| Incident report and investigation record | Mandatory | All | Per incident; investigation as per policy | Manager / Senior staff |
| Safeguarding concern and outcome record | Mandatory when concern raised | All | Per concern; outcome when known | Manager / Safeguarding Lead |
| Complaint record and response | Mandatory when complaint made | All | Per complaint; response within policy timescale | Manager / Complaints lead |
| Lessons learned / action plan (from incidents or complaints) | Mandatory | All | After investigation; follow-up until closed | Manager |
| Restraint / restrictive practice incident review | Mandatory where restraint used | All | Per significant episode; thematic review regularly | Manager / Nurse |

**CQC mapping – Section G**

- **Safe**: Incidents and safeguarding show the service recognises and responds to harm.
- **Well-Led**: Complaints and lessons learned show a learning culture.
- **Responsive**: Complaints are taken seriously and used to improve.

**Why inspectors expect this**: Inspectors look for patterns in incidents, whether safeguarding is reported and followed up, and whether complaints lead to change. Missing or delayed responses are negative findings. These documents are often requested early.

---

### Section H: Governance and Quality

| Document Name | Mandatory / Conditional / Optional | Service Types | Review Frequency | Responsible Role |
|---------------|------------------------------------|---------------|------------------|------------------|
| Care plan / care record audit log | Mandatory | All | At least quarterly per person or sample | Manager / Quality lead |
| Care plan review sign-off and dates | Mandatory | All | Per review (min 12-monthly) | Key Worker / Care Co-ordinator |
| Staff supervision and competency record (reference to folder) | Mandatory | All | Per supervision cycle | Manager |
| Deprivation of Liberty (DoLS / LPS) documentation (where applicable) | Conditional (when authorisation in place) | All | Per authorisation; review before expiry | Manager / DoLS lead / Best Interests Assessor |
| Discharge summary and follow-up plan | Mandatory on discharge | All | Event-triggered | Care Co-ordinator / Key Worker / Consultant |

**CQC mapping – Section H**

- **Well-Led**: Audits and review dates show the service checks quality and acts on it.
- **Effective**: Regular reviews and discharge planning support continuity.
- **Safe**: DoLS/LPS and supervision show lawful, supervised practice.

**Why inspectors expect this**: Inspectors ask for audit results and review dates. Out-of-date reviews and lack of audit evidence are common “Well-Led” limitations. DoLS/LPS must be in place where someone is deprived of liberty.

## 4. Inspector Behaviour: What They Check First, What Often Fails, What Must Be to Hand in Emergencies

### 4.1 Documents Inspectors Usually Check First

Inspectors often start with documents that give a quick picture of safety, planning, and involvement. Staff should ensure these are **current, complete, and easy to find**.

| Document | Why it’s checked early |
|----------|------------------------|
| **Care and support plan (Section B)** | Shows whether care is planned and personalised. |
| **Risk assessment (Section D)** | Core to “Safe”; shows if risks are identified and managed. |
| **Daily / recent care notes (Section C)** | Shows if care is actually delivered and recorded. |
| **Medication record (Section E)** | Quick check for accuracy and recent entries. |
| **Consent / capacity (Section A)** | Ensures care is lawful and person-centred. |
| **Incident and safeguarding (Section G)** | Shows how the service handles harm and concerns. |
| **Care plan review date (Section B / H)** | Shows if plans are kept up to date. |

---

### 4.2 Documents That Commonly Fail Inspections

These are frequently cited when services are rated Requires Improvement or Inadequate. Extra attention to completeness, dates, and consistency is recommended.

| Document / Area | Common failure |
|-----------------|----------------|
| **Risk assessment (Section D)** | Out of date, not personalised, or not updated after incidents. |
| **Care plan review (Section B)** | Review date overdue; no evidence of person or family involvement in review. |
| **Daily care notes (Section C)** | Gaps in recording; notes not reflecting the care plan or the person’s experience. |
| **Restraint and restrictive practice (Sections C and D)** | Not recorded; no clear justification or review; no reduction plan. |
| **Medication (Section E)** | MAR not signed; wrong dose or time; no recent medication review. |
| **Consent and capacity (Section A)** | No clear consent or best interests record; capacity not assessed when it should be. |
| **Safeguarding (Section G)** | Concerns not reported or not followed up; no outcome recorded. |
| **DoLS / LPS (Section H)** | Authorisation expired or missing where someone is deprived of liberty. |
| **Communication and involvement (Section F)** | No communication passport (LD); no evidence of involvement in reviews. |

---

### 4.3 Documents That Must Be Immediately Accessible in Emergencies

In an emergency (e.g. collapse, serious incident, missing person, fire), certain information must be available **without delay** to protect the person and others.

| Document | Why it’s needed in an emergency |
|----------|---------------------------------|
| **Allergies and adverse reactions (Section E)** | To avoid giving a drug or treatment that could cause harm. |
| **Current medication list (Section E)** | So emergency and out-of-hours staff know what the person takes. |
| **Key risks and safety plan (Section D)** | e.g. self-harm, violence, missing person—so staff can act safely. |
| **Who to contact (Section A)** | To inform next of kin or advocate quickly. |
| **Communication passport / key communication needs (Section F)** | So the person can be supported to communicate when distressed. |
| **Personal emergency evacuation plan (Section D)** | To evacuate safely in a fire or other emergency. |
| **Resuscitation / DNAR status (Section E)** | So staff know whether to attempt CPR. |

Services should define “immediately accessible” in policy (e.g. at the front of the folder, in a summary sheet, or in a defined emergency section) and ensure night and weekend staff know where to find it.

---

## 5. Summary: One-Page Reference

- **Sections**: Identity and Consent → Assessment and Planning → Care and Treatment → Risk and Safety → Health and Wellbeing → Involvement and Communication → Incidents, Safeguarding and Complaints → Governance and Quality.
- **Each document** has: name, mandatory/conditional/optional, service type (MH, LD, All), review frequency, responsible role, and CQC domain with “why inspectors expect it”.
- **Inspectors** often look first at: care plan, risk assessment, daily notes, medication, consent, incidents/safeguarding, review dates.
- **Common failures**: out-of-date or generic risk assessments and care plans; gaps in daily notes; poor restraint/safeguarding/DoLS documentation; missing involvement/communication evidence.
- **Emergency access**: allergies, current medication, key risks, contacts, communication needs, PEEP, DNAR/ReSPECT.

This blueprint is the **foundation knowledge** for building a digital CQC readiness system: any system should organise and present information in line with this structure and these rules so that services can demonstrate compliance and inspectors can find the right evidence quickly.

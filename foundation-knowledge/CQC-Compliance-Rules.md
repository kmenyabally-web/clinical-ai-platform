# CQC Compliance Rules: Active Care Folder

**Definitive Compliance Ruleset for CQC Readiness**  
*Derived from the Gold-Standard Active Care Folder Blueprint*

---

## Purpose of This Document

This document converts the Active Care Folder blueprint into **clear, enforceable compliance rules**. It states:

- When each document is required (always or only in certain circumstances)
- How review cycles work and when documents are in date, due, or overdue
- Which documents are emergency-critical and must be instantly accessible
- How much inspection risk each document carries if missing or weak
- Who is accountable and what staff must know on the day of inspection
- What commonly goes wrong and what strong evidence looks like

The rules are written in plain English. They do not assume any particular digital system, database, or software. They are intended to be the **definitive compliance ruleset** against which any system or process can be checked.

---

## How to Use This Document

- **Section 1** gives mandatory vs conditional rules for every document type, with clear IF/THEN logic.
- **Section 2** defines review cycle rules (in date, due, overdue, and event-triggered reviews).
- **Section 3** lists emergency-critical documents and why they must be immediately accessible.
- **Section 4** assigns inspection risk (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW) and describes typical inspector response.
- **Section 5** sets out accountability (primary role, review owner, and “know on the day”).
- **Section 6** describes common failure patterns and weak vs strong evidence.

Documents are grouped by the same sections (A to H) as in the blueprint. Where a rule applies to “all document types” in a section, it is stated once at section level.

## 1. Mandatory vs Conditional Rules

For each document type, the rule states whether it is **ALWAYS mandatory** or **Conditional**. If conditional, the **trigger** (when it is required) and **when it is not required** are stated in IF/THEN form.

---

### Section A: Identity and Consent

| Document Type | Rule Type | Rule in Plain English |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Referral and acceptance record** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST hold a record of how the person was referred and accepted. There are no circumstances in which a person receives care without this. |
| **Consent to care and treatment** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST hold a record that the person has consented to care and treatment (or that care is given under a valid best interests or legal framework). IF the person lacks capacity for a specific decision THEN a capacity assessment and best interests record are required (see below). |
| **Capacity assessment and best interests** | Conditional | IF there is any doubt about the person’s capacity to consent to the care or treatment being offered, OR IF the person has previously been found to lack capacity in a relevant area, THEN a capacity assessment and (where applicable) best interests decision record MUST be in the folder. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person has capacity and has given informed consent, and there is no dispute or doubt about capacity. |
| **Advance decisions / Advance statements** | Conditional | IF the person has made an advance decision to refuse treatment, or holds an advance statement that the service is aware of, THEN a copy or summary MUST be in the folder and staff must know where it is. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person has not made such a decision or the service is not holding one. |
| **Who to contact (next of kin, advocate, GP)** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST hold an up-to-date record of who to contact (e.g. next of kin, advocate if relevant, GP). There are no circumstances where the service provides care without knowing who to contact in an emergency or for involvement. |

### Section B: Assessment and Planning

| Document Type | Rule Type | Rule in Plain English |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Initial assessment (needs, strengths, preferences)** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST complete an initial assessment of needs, strengths, and preferences when the person is admitted or accepted. There are no exceptions. |
| **Care and support plan (or equivalent)** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST have a care and support plan (or equivalent) for every person. IF the person is under a different planning framework (e.g. CPA in mental health), THEN that plan fulfils this requirement provided it contains the same type of information (needs, goals, how care is delivered). |
| **Personalised care plan (daily/weekly goals and activities)** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST have a plan that sets out what the person is working towards and what activities or interventions happen day to day or week to week. This may be part of the main care plan or a separate document. |
| **Mental Health Care Plan / CPA documentation** | Conditional | IF the person receives care under the Care Programme Approach (CPA) or under a mental health care plan that requires such documentation, THEN the folder MUST contain the CPA or equivalent documentation. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person is not on CPA or similar (e.g. some community or non-NHS MH services). |
| **Health Action Plan (or equivalent)** | Conditional | IF the service is for people with a learning disability, THEN a Health Action Plan (or equivalent) is required. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the service is not for people with a learning disability, or when the commissioning body does not require it and the person’s health is fully covered in the main care plan. |
| **Positive Behaviour Support (PBS) plan** | Conditional | IF the person has behaviour that challenges or that requires a structured support plan to reduce restrictive practice, THEN a PBS plan (or equivalent behaviour support plan) MUST be in place. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When there is no such behaviour or when behaviour is fully managed within the main care and risk assessment without a separate plan. |
| **Transition plan (admission, transfer, discharge)** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST have a transition plan when the person is admitted, transferred to another service, or discharged. IF the person is not currently transitioning THEN the last transition record (e.g. admission or last discharge) must still be available. On discharge, a discharge plan or summary is required (see Section H). |

### Section C: Care and Treatment

| Document Type | Rule Type | Rule in Plain English |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Daily care notes / progress notes** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST keep daily (or per-shift) care or progress notes for every person. IF the service is not 24-hour THEN “daily” means on each day the person receives care. There are no exceptions. |
| **Activity and engagement record** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST keep a record of the person’s activities and engagement, at the frequency set in the care plan (e.g. daily or weekly). |
| **Treatment record (psychological, therapy, clinical)** | Conditional | IF the person receives psychological therapy, therapy sessions, or other formal clinical interventions as part of their care, THEN each session or intervention MUST be recorded. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When no such treatment is being delivered. |
| **Seclusion / long-term segregation record** | Conditional | IF the service has used seclusion or long-term segregation for this person, THEN a record of each episode and its review MUST be in the folder. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the service does not use these practices or has not used them for this person. |
| **Restraint and restrictive practice record** | Conditional | IF restraint or any restrictive practice has been used for this person, THEN each episode MUST be recorded and there MUST be a record of review and (where applicable) a plan to reduce use. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When restraint or restrictive practice has never been used for this person. |
| **Food and fluid record** | Conditional | IF there is a known risk related to eating or drinking (e.g. eating disorder, underweight, swallowing difficulty, refusal), THEN a food and fluid record MUST be kept for as long as the risk is actively managed. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When there is no such risk. |
| **Sleep and behaviour chart** | Conditional | IF the care plan or risk assessment requires monitoring of sleep or behaviour (e.g. to inform medication or safety), THEN a sleep and behaviour chart (or equivalent) MUST be kept. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When it is not part of the agreed plan. |

### Section D: Risk and Safety

| Document Type | Rule Type | Rule in Plain English |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Risk assessment (combined or multi-domain)** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST have a current risk assessment that covers the person’s relevant risks (e.g. to self, others, from environment). There are no exceptions. |
| **Self-harm and suicide risk assessment** | Conditional | IF the person is in a mental health service or has a known or suspected risk of self-harm or suicide, THEN a specific self-harm and suicide risk assessment MUST be in place and updated as per policy. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When there is no such risk and the service type does not routinely require it. |
| **Violence and aggression risk assessment** | Conditional | IF the person has presented or is known to present a risk of violence or aggression to others, THEN a violence and aggression risk assessment MUST be in place. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When no such risk has been identified. |
| **Safeguarding risk assessment** | Conditional | IF a safeguarding concern has been raised about or involving this person, THEN a safeguarding risk assessment (and outcome when known) MUST be in the folder. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When no safeguarding concern has been raised. |
| **Fire safety and evacuation (PEEP)** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST have a personal emergency evacuation plan (PEEP) or equivalent for every person, so that in a fire or other emergency staff know how to evacuate them safely. |
| **Missing person risk and protocol** | Conditional | IF the person uses community leave, goes out alone, or has gone missing before (or is otherwise at risk of going missing), THEN a missing person risk and protocol MUST be in place. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person does not leave the premises or when there is no identified missing person risk. |
| **Environmental risk assessment** | Conditional | IF the person is in a residential or ward setting, THEN an environmental risk assessment for their living environment MUST be in place. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person is not in a residential or ward setting (e.g. outreach only). |

### Section E: Health and Wellbeing

| Document Type | Rule Type | Rule in Plain English |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Medication record (MAR / prescription chart)** | Conditional | IF the person is prescribed and receives any medication from or through the service, THEN a medication administration record (MAR) or prescription chart MUST be in place and used for every administration. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person does not receive any medication from the service. |
| **Medication review record** | Conditional | IF the person is on medication, THEN there MUST be a record of medication review at the frequency required by policy (e.g. at least every 12 months; more often for psychotropic medication). WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person is not on any medication. |
| **Physical health check and screening record** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST hold or have access to a record of physical health checks and screening as per national or local guidelines (e.g. annual health check for people with a learning disability). For other services, evidence of at least annual consideration of physical health is required. |
| **Health passport (or equivalent)** | Conditional | IF the person has a learning disability, THEN a health passport (or equivalent) is strongly recommended and often expected by inspectors; if the service or commissioner has adopted it as standard, THEN it is required. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the service does not use health passports and the person’s health information is fully and clearly set out elsewhere. |
| **Allergies and adverse reactions** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST hold a record of known allergies and significant adverse reactions. IF the person has none known, THEN that must be explicitly recorded (e.g. “No known allergies”). |
| **DNAR / ReSPECT form** | Conditional | IF the person has a Do Not Attempt Resuscitation (DNAR) decision or a ReSPECT form, AND the service holds or is aware of it, THEN a copy or clear summary MUST be in the folder. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When there is no DNAR or ReSPECT in place. |
| **Diet and nutrition plan** | Conditional | IF the person has a specific diet or nutrition need (e.g. diabetes, swallowing difficulty, weight management), THEN a diet and nutrition plan MUST be in place. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When there are no such needs. |

### Section F: Involvement and Communication

| Document Type | Rule Type | Rule in Plain English |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Communication passport / profile** | Conditional (mandatory for LD) | IF the person has a learning disability, THEN a communication passport or profile is mandatory. IF the person has other communication needs (e.g. language, sensory, cognitive) that staff need to know to deliver safe and person-centred care, THEN a communication passport or profile (or equivalent) is required. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person has no learning disability and no communication needs that require a separate document. |
| **Involvement and participation record** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST be able to show that the person (and where appropriate their family or advocate) has been involved in reviews and in decisions about their care. Evidence must exist for at least the most recent care plan review and ideally for ongoing participation. |
| **Relatives and carer contact and involvement log** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST keep a record of contact and involvement with relatives and carers (who they are, when contacted, what was discussed). This may be part of care notes or a separate log. |
| **Advocacy referral and contact record** | Conditional | IF an advocate is involved for this person, THEN the folder MUST contain a record of the advocacy referral and of contact with the advocate. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When no advocate is involved. |
| **Accessible information record** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST record how the person prefers to receive information (e.g. easy read, large print, interpreter, language). IF the person has no specific requirements THEN “standard format” or similar must be recorded. |

### Section G: Incidents, Safeguarding and Complaints

| Document Type | Rule Type | Rule in Plain English |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Incident report and investigation record** | Conditional | IF an incident has occurred that affects or involves this person (and that the service records as an incident), THEN an incident report MUST exist and, where policy requires it, an investigation record. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When no such incident has occurred. |
| **Safeguarding concern and outcome record** | Conditional | IF a safeguarding concern has been raised about or involving this person, THEN a record of the concern and (when known) the outcome MUST be in the folder. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When no safeguarding concern has been raised. |
| **Complaint record and response** | Conditional | IF the person (or someone on their behalf) has made a complaint, THEN a record of the complaint and the response MUST be in the folder. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When no complaint has been made. |
| **Lessons learned / action plan** | Conditional | IF an incident or complaint has led to an investigation or formal review, THEN there MUST be a record of lessons learned and any action plan, and of whether actions have been completed. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When there has been no such investigation. |
| **Restraint / restrictive practice incident review** | Conditional | IF restraint or restrictive practice has been used, THEN each significant episode MUST be reviewed and there MUST be a record of that review. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When restraint has not been used. |

### Section H: Governance and Quality

| Document Type | Rule Type | Rule in Plain English |
|---------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Care plan / care record audit log** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST be able to show that care plans or care records are audited (e.g. at least quarterly, by sample or per person). A log or record of audits and findings must exist. |
| **Care plan review sign-off and dates** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST hold a clear record of when the care plan was last reviewed and who signed it off. The next review date should be visible. |
| **Staff supervision and competency record** | ALWAYS mandatory | The service MUST be able to show that staff who deliver care receive supervision and that competency is assessed. This may be held at service level with reference to the folder; the folder or system must support the link between the person and the staff caring for them. |
| **Deprivation of Liberty (DoLS / LPS) documentation** | Conditional | IF the person is, or may be, deprived of their liberty in the sense of the Mental Capacity Act (e.g. under DoLS or Liberty Protection Safeguards), THEN the folder MUST contain the current authorisation and evidence of review before expiry. WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person is not subject to a deprivation of liberty authorisation. |
| **Discharge summary and follow-up plan** | Conditional | IF the person has been discharged from the service, THEN a discharge summary and follow-up plan MUST be in the folder (or in the closed record). WHEN NOT REQUIRED: When the person is still receiving care and has not been discharged. |

## 2. Review Cycle Rules

For each document type, the table below defines:

- **Expected review frequency**
- **In date**: when the document is considered current
- **Due for review**: when a review should be scheduled or in progress
- **Overdue**: when the document is no longer compliant
- **Event-triggered**: when a review MUST happen after a specific event (incident, risk change, capacity, medication, placement)

---

### Section A: Identity and Consent

| Document | Review Frequency | In Date | Due for Review | Overdue | Event-Triggered Review |
|----------|------------------|---------|----------------|---------|------------------------|
| Referral and acceptance record | Event only | N/A (one-off at referral) | N/A | N/A | On admission or acceptance only. |
| Consent to care and treatment | When care changes | Consent is current and covers current care | When care or treatment changes | When care has changed and consent has not been re-checked | After any significant change in care or treatment. |
| Capacity assessment and best interests | When circumstances change | Assessment is recent and circumstances unchanged | When person’s condition or situation changes | When capacity is in doubt and no recent assessment | After change in mental state, placement, or treatment; after incident that may affect capacity. |
| Advance decisions / statements | When person requests | Document reflects person’s current wishes | When person indicates a change | When person says it is out of date | When person requests a change. |
| Who to contact | At least annually | Updated within last 12 months | Within 3 months of 12-month date | More than 12 months since update | When person or family says contact details have changed. |

### Section B: Assessment and Planning

|----------|------------------|---------|----------------|---------|------------------------|
| Initial assessment | On admission; full re-assessment annually | Done at admission; re-assessment within 12 months | Re-assessment due within 3 months of 12-month date | Re-assessment more than 12 months since admission or last full assessment | After major change in needs or placement. |
| Care and support plan | At least every 12 months | Last review within 12 months | Review due within 3 months of 12-month date | Last review more than 12 months ago | After significant change in needs, risk, or placement; after serious incident. |

| Health Action Plan | Annual; when health changes | Reviewed within 12 months | Due within 3 months of 12-month date | More than 12 months since review | When health or medication changes significantly. |
| PBS plan | As per plan; at least annually | Formal review within 12 months | Due within 3 months of 12-month date | More than 12 months since formal review | After serious behaviour incident; when restraint increases; when placement or environment changes. |

### Summary: Event-Triggered Reviews

Documents that **must** be reviewed or updated after specific events:

| Event | Documents That Must Be Reviewed or Updated |
|-------|--------------------------------------------|
| **After an incident** | Risk assessment; incident report and investigation; restraint record if restraint was used; lessons learned/action plan. |
| **After a change in risk** | Risk assessment; self-harm/suicide risk if applicable; violence and aggression risk if applicable; care and support plan. |
| **After a change in capacity** | Capacity assessment and best interests; consent record; DoLS/LPS if applicable. |
| **After a change in medication** | Medication record (MAR); medication review; health passport if in use; allergies if new reaction. |
| **After a change in placement or environment** | Transition plan; PEEP; environmental risk assessment; risk assessment; care and support plan; DoLS/LPS if applicable. |

## 3. Emergency-Critical Tagging

The following documents **must** be **immediately accessible** in an emergency and **instantly understandable** by staff who do not know the person (e.g. bank staff, night staff, emergency services). Services should keep these in a defined place (e.g. front of folder, single “emergency summary” sheet, or clearly marked section) and ensure all staff know where to find them.

| Document | Why It Is Emergency-Critical | Why It Must Be Instantly Understandable |
|----------|------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Allergies and adverse reactions** | To avoid giving a drug or treatment that could cause serious harm or death. | Unfamiliar staff or paramedics must see at a glance what to avoid; no time to search the folder. |
| **Current medication list** | So emergency and out-of-hours staff know what the person takes and can avoid dangerous interactions or omissions. | In a collapse or crisis, someone must be able to read the list quickly and accurately. |
| **Key risks and safety plan (summary)** | So staff can act safely (e.g. self-harm, violence, missing person) without reading the full risk assessment. | In a crisis, staff need the main risks and “what to do” in one place. |
| **Who to contact (next of kin, advocate, GP)** | To inform the right people quickly and to get clinical information (e.g. from GP) if needed. | Contact details must be visible without searching; wrong or outdated details can delay support. |
| **Communication passport or key communication needs** | So the person can be supported to communicate when distressed; reduces risk of misunderstanding and escalation. | Unfamiliar staff must know how to communicate with the person (e.g. simple language, no jargon, use of pictures). |
| **Personal emergency evacuation plan (PEEP)** | To evacuate the person safely in a fire or other emergency. | In a fire, staff must know immediately how to get the person out (e.g. equipment, route, support needed). |
| **Resuscitation / DNAR status** | So staff know whether to attempt CPR; acting against a valid DNAR can cause harm and distress. | Must be obvious so that in a cardiac arrest the right decision is made in seconds. |

**Rule**: Any system or process that holds the Active Care Folder must support the idea that these seven areas are **emergency-critical**: they must be available in one place or one click, in a form that can be read and understood in under a minute by someone who has never seen the folder before.

## 4. Inspection Risk Weighting

For each document type, inspection risk is classified as **HIGH**, **MEDIUM**, or **LOW** when the document is **missing or out of date**. The table also describes how inspectors typically respond.

**Definitions:**

- **HIGH RISK**: Often leads to limiting the rating (e.g. “Requires Improvement”) or contributing to “Inadequate” in the relevant domain. Inspectors are very likely to look at these and to cite them in the report if weak or missing.
- **MEDIUM RISK**: Can lead to negative findings and recommendations. Inspectors often check these; gaps are noted and may affect the rating if combined with other issues.
- **LOW RISK**: Less often the main cause of a poor rating. Gaps may be mentioned as improvement areas but are less likely alone to drive “Inadequate” or “Requires Improvement”.

---

### Section A: Identity and Consent

| Document | Inspection Risk if Missing or Weak | How Inspectors Typically Respond |
|----------|------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Referral and acceptance record | MEDIUM | Expect to see how the person came into the service. If missing, they may question governance and whether the service knows who it is caring for. |
| Consent to care and treatment | HIGH | Core to lawful care. If missing or unclear, inspectors will question whether care is consent-based or properly in best interests. Can directly affect Safe and Effective. |
| Capacity assessment and best interests | HIGH (when required) | If capacity is in doubt and there is no assessment or best interests record, inspectors treat this as a serious gap. Can affect Safe and Effective. |
| Advance decisions / statements | LOW (when held) | If the service holds one but it is not recorded or findable, inspectors may note poor organisation; less often a primary cause of poor rating. |
| Who to contact | MEDIUM | If missing or wrong, inspectors note risk in emergency and involvement. Often raised as a recommendation. |

|----------|------------------------------------|----------------------------------|

| Diet and nutrition plan | MEDIUM (when needed) | If need exists and there is no plan, inspectors note risk; often linked to health and safety. |

## 5. Accountability Rules

For each document type, the table below states:

- **Primary responsible role**: the role that is responsible for the document existing and being correct.
- **Accountable for ensuring it is reviewed**: the role that must ensure review happens on time (may be the same or a manager).
- **What inspectors expect staff to “know on the day”**: what the responsible person (or staff asked by the inspector) should be able to say or show without having to look it up in detail.

## 6. Common Failure Patterns

This section describes **why** documents commonly fail inspection and what **weak** evidence looks like versus **strong** evidence. It is based on typical CQC findings and helps services avoid the same mistakes.

---

### Section A: Identity and Consent

| Document | Common Reasons It Fails Inspection | Weak Evidence | Strong Evidence |
|----------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Consent to care and treatment | Consent not recorded; consent recorded once and never updated when care changed; consent assumed rather than documented. | Single undated signature; no record of what was consented to; consent from years ago with no re-check. | Dated record of what the person consented to; re-check when care changed; signed and dated by person or advocate. |
| Capacity assessment and best interests | No assessment when capacity was in doubt; best interests not recorded; decision not clearly linked to the specific decision. | Verbal “we think they have capacity”; no written best interests; generic wording. | Written capacity assessment for the relevant decision; dated best interests record with reasons and who was involved. |
| Who to contact | Out-of-date details; contact only in staff head or on a whiteboard; wrong person named. | No single list; old phone numbers; “family” with no name or number. | Up-to-date list in the folder; named contacts with phone numbers; reviewed at least annually and when person says. |

---

### Section B: Assessment and Planning

| Document | Common Reasons It Fails Inspection | Weak Evidence | Strong Evidence |
|----------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Care and support plan | Plan overdue for review; plan generic (could apply to anyone); no evidence of person’s involvement in review. | Review date more than 12 months ago; copy-paste goals; no signature or date from person or family. | Review within 12 months; goals and language that reflect the person; evidence of involvement (e.g. signature, meeting note); next review date clear. |
| Risk assessment | Assessment out of date; not updated after incident; generic (same wording for many people); no clear plan to reduce risk. | “Reviewed annually” but last review 18 months ago; same risks for everyone; no individual triggers or actions. | Dated within 12 months (or after incident); personalised risks and triggers; clear actions and who does them; plan to reduce where possible. |
| PBS plan (when required) | No plan despite behaviour that challenges; plan not reviewed; no clear strategy to reduce restrictive practice. | Plan exists but is generic; no review in over a year; restraint use not linked to plan. | Person-specific strategies; regular review; restraint linked to plan and reducing over time. |

---

### Section C: Care and Treatment

| Document | Common Reasons It Fails Inspection | Weak Evidence | Strong Evidence |
|----------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Daily care notes | Gaps (days or shifts with no note); notes too brief or vague; notes not reflecting the care plan; notes written long after the event. | “All fine”; “As usual”; whole week missing; notes written in one block at end of week. | Note for every day/shift care was given; what was done and how the person was; written at the time or same day; links to plan where relevant. |
| Restraint and restrictive practice record | Episode not recorded; no review after use; no plan to reduce; justification not clear. | Restraint used but no entry; “see care plan” with no detail; no trend or monthly review. | Every episode recorded with date, time, reason, who was involved; review after each significant episode; monthly trend and reduction plan. |

---

### Section D: Risk and Safety

| Document | Common Reasons It Fails Inspection | Weak Evidence | Strong Evidence |
|----------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Risk assessment | Out of date; generic; not updated after incident or change; no ownership. | Same template for everyone; last review 2 years ago; incident last month but assessment unchanged. | Dated; person-specific risks and triggers; updated after incident or change; clear responsible person and review date. |
| PEEP | Missing in residential/ward; not updated when mobility or premises changed; staff don’t know where it is. | No PEEP; PEEP from when person could walk (now uses wheelchair); kept in office not with person. | PEEP for every person; reflects current mobility and building; staff can find it immediately; reviewed when things change. |

---

### Section E: Health and Wellbeing

| Document | Common Reasons It Fails Inspection | Weak Evidence | Strong Evidence |
|----------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Medication record (MAR) | Unsigned administrations; wrong dose or time; gaps; no record of errors or refusals. | Blank spaces; signatures missing; “given” with no time; no follow-up when refused. | Every administration signed and dated; correct dose and time; refusals or errors recorded and followed up. |
| Medication review | No review for over 12 months; psychotropic medication not reviewed at 6 months; no record of who reviewed. | “GP will review” with no date; last review 2 years ago; no evidence of review. | Dated review within policy period; who did it (GP/consultant/pharmacist); next review date; psychotropic medication reviewed as per policy. |
| Allergies | Not recorded; recorded in one place but not checked; “none” not stated so staff assume unknown. | No allergy section; allergy in notes but not on MAR or summary; outdated. | Clear list (or “No known allergies”); checked at each review; visible in emergency summary. |

---

### Section F: Involvement and Communication

| Document | Common Reasons It Fails Inspection | Weak Evidence | Strong Evidence |
|----------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Communication passport (LD) | Missing in LD service; not updated when communication changed; staff don’t use it. | No passport; passport from years ago; “can communicate” with no detail. | Up-to-date; how to communicate (words, signs, pictures); what helps and what doesn’t; used in handovers. |
| Involvement and participation | No evidence person was involved in review; review done without person or family. | Review form signed by staff only; no record of meeting with person; “discussed with family” with no date or detail. | Record of meeting with person (and family where appropriate); signature or clear note of involvement; date and who was there. |

### Section G: Incidents, Safeguarding and Complaints

| Document | Common Reasons It Fails Inspection | Weak Evidence | Strong Evidence |
|----------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------|
| Incident report and investigation | Incident not reported; investigation not done within policy time; no outcome or action. | Incident known but no form; investigation started but never finished; no date. | Report completed at time of incident; investigation within policy; outcome and actions recorded; actions followed up. |

### Section H: Governance and Quality

| DoLS / LPS | Authorisation expired; no authorisation when person is deprived of liberty; not reviewed before expiry. | Expired 2 months ago; “we’re waiting”; no record in folder. | Current authorisation in folder; expiry date clear; review before expiry; renewal in progress if needed. |
| Care plan / care record audit log | No audit; audit done but no log or actions; audits not regular. | “We check plans” with no record; last audit 18 months ago; no actions. | Audit at least quarterly; log of what was audited and when; findings and actions; actions closed or tracked. |

## 7. Summary: Rules at a Glance

- **Mandatory vs conditional**: Each document type has a clear rule. Conditional documents have an IF (trigger) and WHEN NOT REQUIRED.
- **Review cycles**: Each document has defined “in date”, “due for review”, and “overdue”. Event-triggered reviews are required after incident, risk change, capacity change, medication change, or placement change where specified.
- **Emergency-critical**: Seven areas must be immediately accessible and instantly understandable: allergies, current medication, key risks, who to contact, communication needs, PEEP, DNAR/ReSPECT.
- **Inspection risk**: HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW is assigned per document; HIGH items are those that most often drive “Requires Improvement” or “Inadequate” when missing or weak.
- **Accountability**: Primary role, review owner, and “know on the day” are set per document so that staff and managers know who is responsible and what they must be able to say or show.
- **Common failures**: For each high-risk or often-failed document, common reasons for failure and the difference between weak and strong evidence are described.

These rules form the **definitive compliance ruleset** for the Active Care Folder. Any process or system that supports CQC readiness should align with these rules so that evidence is complete, in date, and inspection-ready.
